# The Session: solo Model UN campaign (rules v0.2)

Status: **rules v0.1 approved by Ben on 5 Nov 2026**. v0.2 adds the changes the balance simulation called for
(section 17). Ben is the overall designer and Claude the lead programmer. Phase 1 (the engine and the balance
harness) is built; nothing in the interface is built until Ben has seen the balance numbers.

Ben's brief, in his words:
- "sound effects, animations, flow, setup, menus, the entire lot";
- "an hour long experience, that feels like a combination of risk, monopoly, civ games, with depth, gravity, decision making, unique scenarios";
- "still keeping it educational, and informative";
- "chapters to a summit getting the majority of the resolution ready, then the summit actually being voting and higher frequency decision making";
- "resolutions often have clauses added or entirely removed";
- "reward strategy … different mechanics rewarding different types of players";
- "respect or influence meters growing".

Decided on 5 Nov:
- **Scope:** solo only. The live class committee stays procedure-only (D-091).
- **Luck:** event cards and the vote's real uncertainty only; no dice.
- **Guests:** the game is kept on that laptop only, and nothing goes to the server.
- **Clauses:** clause cards, which the student may reword in their own words.
- **Winning:** several victory paths.
- **Meters:** Influence, Respect, Trust (per country) and Expertise.

The game borrows the *feel* of territory, economy and civilisation games. Its mechanics, names and look are its
own: the war-room board the platform already has.

---

## 1. Pillars

1. **Every mechanic is real diplomacy.** Each rule maps to something delegates actually do: lobbying, blocs,
   sponsors and signatories, amendments, division of the question, promises. If a rule teaches nothing true, it goes.
2. **Strategy beats luck.** Randomness comes only from event cards and the honest uncertainty of a vote. A good
   plan, well executed, should usually win.
3. **More than one way to play well.** A broker, a hardliner, a drafter and an analyst can all finish proud,
   on different victory paths.
4. **Gravity.** The countries are real, the positions carry sources, and the stakes are stated plainly. Effects
   make moments land; they never turn the topic into a joke.
5. **Honest.**
   - Positions are labelled estimates with their sources.
   - Events on real-issue scenarios are marked *hypothetical*.
   - Whip reads are scored against the votes actually cast in the game.
   - The game never writes the student's words.

## 2. The shape of an hour

| Part | What happens | Standard length |
|---|---|---|
| Title and setup | Scenario, country, length, rival strength, tutorial on or off | 3 min |
| **Chapter 1: Working group** | The draft has a few clauses and gaps. Add, cut and reword clauses; find allies. | 13 min |
| **Chapter 2: Blocs** | Regional groups and blocs caucus. Make promises, win group endorsements, take on the rival drafts. | 13 min |
| **Chapter 3: Sponsors** | Lock the text, gather sponsors and signatories, and table the draft before the rivals table theirs. | 13 min |
| **The Summit** | Opening speech, then clause-by-clause votes, floor amendments, points and motions: quick decisions, faster pace. | 15 min |
| Debrief dossier | Victory paths, what moved and why, every country's position with sources | 3 min |

Lengths:
- **Quick:** one combined chapter and the summit, about 25 minutes.
- **Standard:** three chapters and the summit, about 60 minutes.
- **Full:** three longer chapters and a longer summit, about 80 minutes.

Any game can be saved and resumed between turns.

## 3. Meters

| Meter | What it is | Goes up | Goes down | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Influence** (spent) | Political capital: the economy | Income each turn, plus more for group endorsements, a good speech and accurate whip reads | Spending it | Pays for lobbying, side events and calling in favours |
| **Respect** (kept) | Your standing in the room | Kept promises, good procedure, a gracious loss, a speech that suits the room | Broken promises, procedural mistakes, attacking allies | Makes lobbying cheaper. At set levels you're invited to chair a caucus or be lead drafter. |
| **Trust** (one per country) | What each delegation remembers about you | Support given, promises kept | Promises broken, voting against an ally's clause | Shown as a ring on the country's token. High trust makes a country open to a favour; low trust closes it to you. |
| **Expertise** (kept) | What you've learned | Reading a country file, the right point or motion at a procedure moment, accurate reads | (never) | Unlocks diplomatic skills (section 7) |

## 4. A chapter turn

A chapter has four turns. Each turn runs in this order:
1. **News:** an event card is drawn and shown as breaking news.
2. **Your actions:** 3 action points (AP).
3. **Rivals move:** visibly, on the map.
4. **Upkeep:** income comes in, meters change, and the chapter objective is checked.

| Action | AP | Influence | Effect | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lobby** a delegation | 1 | Cost rises with how far the country is from you on that commitment; Trust and Respect lower it | You hear its real line (from its evidence), then try to move it on one commitment | Positions have reasons; some can't be moved |
| **Read a country file** | 1 (once each) | 0 | Its positions, sources, blocs and red lines. Expertise rises. | Research before you lobby |
| **Propose a clause** | 1 | 1 | Play a clause card onto the drafting table, optionally reworded. It enters the draft if enough delegations support it. | How text gets into a resolution |
| **Amend / strike / merge** | 1 | 1–2 | Change what a clause commits countries to, cut it, or combine two clauses | Clauses are added and removed all the time |
| **Promise** | 0 | 0 | Back another delegation's clause or vote. Trust rises now; the promise is recorded. Breaking it costs Trust and Respect. | Deals have a price |
| **Side event** | 2 | 3 | A small shift across one regional group | Coalition work outside the room |
| **Call in a favour** | 1 | 0 | A high-trust delegation does what you ask once, then its Trust resets | Relationships are a resource |
| **Whip check** | 1 | 0 | Record what you think a delegation will do. It's scored later against the actual vote: Expertise, and Kingmaker progress. | Reading the room |

**Chapter objectives:**
- **Chapter 1:** have at least *n* clauses in the draft.
- **Chapter 2:** win at least one group endorsement.
- **Chapter 3:** sponsors and signatories to table the draft.

Each objective earns Influence and Respect. Failing one doesn't end the game; it makes the summit harder. For
example, if the draft isn't tabled first, the rivals' draft is voted on first.

**Group endorsements.** If most of a UN regional group leans your way, that group endorses your draft. It gives a
bonus each turn and a token seal on the map. This is the territory layer: the map fills as you win groups over.

## 5. The summit

The pace speeds up and decisions come one after another:
1. **Opening speech.** Choose a stance (firm, open or bridge) and a tone. Words are optional and never marked.
   The tone sets Respect with each bloc.
2. **Division of the question.** The draft is voted on clause by clause. Before each vote, a decision card comes up:
   - accept a rival's floor amendment;
   - make a last offer to one delegation;
   - raise a point of order;
   - use a skill.
3. **Optional decision timer** (on by default in Standard, off in the accessible setting). The Chair calls, and you
   have 20 seconds to decide.
4. **Final vote on the resolution as a whole,** with the board's vote sweep and seal.

## 6. Rivals

Two rival delegations (three on Hard) with opposing secret mandates.
- They propose their own clauses, lobby, make promises and race to table their own draft.
- Their moves show on the map between your turns: "Delegation of X lobbies Kenya".
- Difficulty sets their action points and how well they plan.
- They follow the same rules as the player. No cheating AI.

## 7. Diplomatic skills (unlocked with Expertise)

Four branches, each two or three steps. Students choose what to unlock, so the tree is where a style shows.

| Branch | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Procedure** | Point of order; Division of the question (split a rival's clause); Motion to close debate early |
| **Diplomacy** | Bloc whip (lobby a whole bloc at a reduced cost); Back channel (one secret promise rivals can't see) |
| **Drafting** | Friendly amendment (a free reword when the sponsor agrees); Merge (combine two clauses for 1 AP) |
| **Analysis** | See a rival's next move; a narrower whip dial; spot a delegation's red line before lobbying it |

## 8. Victory paths

The dossier grades each path bronze, silver or gold. The student chooses which to chase, and the setup screen
says what each path rewards.

| Path | Rewards | Suits |
|---|---|---|
| **Mandate** | Your secret instructions are in the final text, and it passes | The hardliner |
| **Consensus** | It passes with the widest support: yes votes, and endorsements across regional groups | The broker |
| **Architect** | The final text is mostly yours: your clause cards, your amendments | The drafter |
| **Kingmaker** | Accurate whip reads, kept promises, high Respect at the end | The analyst |

## 9. Clause cards and the student's own words

- Each scenario has a deck of clause cards: about 10–14, including the draft's own clauses. A card's commitments
  are the scenario's tags, so the vote engine already knows who supports what.
- **Rewording.** The student may reword a card. The engine judges the clause only by the commitments ticked,
  never by the wording, and the debrief says so. Reworded text is kept with the saved game and erased with the
  student.
- **Cutting.** A clause can be cut from the draft. Cutting a clause another delegation wanted costs Trust with it.

## 10. Events

| Kind | Examples | Labelled |
|---|---|---|
| Procedural (every scenario) | The speakers list closes; a point of order is upheld; a caucus overruns | — |
| From the scenario | Its `news` items, and new `campaign.events` | Fictional scenarios: as they are. Real-issue scenarios: **"Hypothetical"** |

An event changes something for a turn: costs, a bloc's mood, an action's price. Cards are drawn from a seeded
deck, so a saved game replays the same way.

## 11. Look, sound and flow

- **Menus:**
  - a title screen on the war-room table with the UN seal;
  - New session / Continue / Almanac / Settings;
  - a pause menu.
- **Setup:**
  - scenario cards;
  - country chosen on the map, with a difficulty rating: an isolated position is hard;
  - length;
  - rival strength;
  - the victory path to chase;
  - tutorial on or off.
- **Almanac:** country files with sources, a procedure guide and a glossary. It's open any time, and reading it
  earns Expertise.
- **Animations:**
  - tokens lean and their Trust rings fill;
  - endorsement seals stamp onto regional groups;
  - event cards flip;
  - clause cards slide onto the drafting table and are torn when struck;
  - the vote sweeps the map;
  - meters tick up.
  - Everything respects reduced motion.
- **Sound:**
  - Web Audio only, like the live room (D-089), with no audio files: gavel, paper, stamp, an influence chime,
    card flip, map sweep, vote ticks, and an optional low murmur of the room.
  - Every sound has a caption.
  - Sound is off until the player turns it on: school laptops.
- **Accessibility:**
  - keyboard play throughout;
  - text size setting;
  - decision timer off in the accessible setting;
  - axe-clean menus.

## 12. Saves and privacy

- **Signed-in student:**
  - the game is saved on the server in a new table (`campaign_save`), after every turn;
  - only that student can open it;
  - reworded clauses go into `erase_student`.
  - Open question for Ben: should teachers see students' results?
- **Guest:** the game is kept in the browser on that laptop. Each move is sent with the game state and checked by
  the server against a signature, so it can't be edited, and nothing about the guest is stored.
- The game uses only first names, as now.

## 14. Balance targets (measured before any interface is built)

- **Standard difficulty:** a competent bot reaches at least silver on its chosen path in **40–60%** of games.
- **Every style has a path:** each bot style (broker, hardliner, drafter, analyst) gets gold on its own path in
  ≥ 30% of games, and none gets it in > 70%.
- **No dominant action:** no single action is more than 35% of a winning bot's moves.
- **Scenarios:** every pack is winnable on every difficulty; the hardest pack on Standard should be ≥ 25%.
- **Length:** turns × a thinking-time model ≈ the target minutes for each length setting.

## 17. What the balance simulation changed (v0.2, 5 Nov 2026)

`python tools/campaign_sim.py --games 20` ran 2,400 games: 8 scenarios, 3 difficulties and 5 bot styles, 20 games
each, at Standard length. The early runs found problems, and each was fixed by a rule that is also truer to life:

| Problem found | Rule added |
|---|---|
| Rivals struck or amended away the player's key clause on the last turn, with no chance to reply. | **The working group decides.** A clause can be struck only if it wouldn't pass now. Someone else's clause can be amended only if the new version wins at least as many yes votes. The same rule applies to the player and to the rivals. |
| The same problem, from turn order. | **Rivals move at the start of each turn**, so the player always has the last word in a chapter. |
| Difficulty made almost no difference. | Rivals now get 1, 2 or 3 actions a turn with 1, 2 or 3 rivals (easy, standard, hard), and their lobbying is weaker or stronger to match. |
| A rival that is a permanent member vetoed every clause in Security Council scenarios. | **Rivals are never permanent members where there is a veto.** The five still vote from their modelled positions and can be lobbied. |
| A random player earned Mandate medals when the starting draft happened to suit them. | **Instructions have three parts:** get two commitments in and keep one out. Gold needs all three in a resolution that passes. |
| Drafting was too easy to win. | **Architect gold** needs most of the final text to be yours, at least three of your own clauses adopted, and 60% of members voting yes. |
| A Standard game was about 45 minutes. | **Five turns a chapter on Standard** (six on Full). |

### Measured results (20 games per cell)

| Target (section 14) | Measured | Met? |
|---|---|---|
| Standard: a competent bot reaches at least silver on its chosen path in 40–60% of games | **63%** | Just over |
| Each style gets gold on its own path in 30–70% of games (Standard) | hardliner **34%**, analyst **37%**, broker **39%**, drafter **69%** | Yes (the drafter is at the edge) |
| No single action is more than 35% of a winning bot's moves | highest is lobbying at **19%** | Yes |
| The hardest scenario on Standard ≥ 25% | Climate change and international security: **25%** | Just |
| No game gets stuck | **0** of 2,400 | Yes |
| A random player does much worse than a competent one | random silver or better **24%**, against 63% | Yes |
| A Standard game takes about an hour | a model of **55–61 minutes** (setup, moves and debrief) | Needs a real play test |

Difficulty now matters. On Easy, a hardliner reaches silver or better 74% of the time; on Hard, 29%. For a broker
the figures are 85% and 52%.

**By scenario** (silver or better, competent bots, easy / standard / hard):

| Scenario | Easy | Standard | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comet Halcyon-7 | 97% | 96% | 91% |
| The situation in Veloria | 86% | 81% | 68% |
| Who should govern AI? | 84% | 78% | 64% |
| Autonomous weapons | 88% | 74% | 59% |
| Migration | 75% | 61% | 60% |
| Moving away from fossil fuels | 75% | 56% | 46% |
| AI and cyber threats to peace (Security Council) | 50% | 33% | 28% |
| Climate change and international security (Security Council) | 30% | 25% | 42% |

These differences come from the scenarios' content, not the rules. Comet Halcyon-7 is a gentle fictional
introduction. The two Security Council scenarios are hard because the permanent members' recorded positions
lead to real vetoes; the way through is bundling a commitment a permanent member wants, so it abstains instead.

**Limits of these numbers:** bots are not students. The time is a model of reading and thinking time, and must be
checked in a real play test. The bots don't use the Almanac, music or their own words, which change nothing in the
rules.

## 20. The dynamic hour and the Chief of Protocol (phase 7, 5 Nov 2026)

Ben chose all four dynamics ("60 min game should be dynamic") and a guide: "a lot more of a walkthrough, with a checkbox to dismiss all tutorial … some kind of character who explains everything … The player should know in full how the game is going to look before it happens." His choices: the Chief of Protocol; the guide also speaks at key moments; the walkthrough runs at the start of every new session until it's turned off.

**Chapter crises** (`content.CRISES`, `engine._open_chapter`). Each chapter opens with one, shown on the chapter card and on the Brief, and it lasts for that chapter:
- *A new government*: one delegation's position on one commitment moves 0.8 towards the opposite of its stated position.
- *The press turns on part of the draft*: every non-rival delegation cools by 0.25 on one commitment.
- *The deadline is brought forward*: the chapter has one turn fewer (`engine.turns_now`).
- *A rival gains a partner*: that rival has one more action each turn.
- *A donor conference*: +4 Influence for you and every rival.
- *A regional group holds a summit*: that group endorses with a lean of 0.2 instead of 0.3.

Each crisis says what it teaches. At each chapter start one or two capitals also send new instructions (±0.4 on one commitment).

**Shifting delegations** (`g["drift"]`, `engine.drift`, `shift`, `mu_of`). A shift is added to the model's position in `latent`, so it reaches the vote, the country file ("Since the session began: cooler towards …"), lobbying costs, promises and offers. Shifts are capped at ±1.5 and logged in the Record.

**Offers at your door** (`engine._make_offers`, `a_offer`). One or two delegations come with a deal each turn, which lapses when the turn ends. Answering costs no action. The deals:
- *Back our clause*: a promise on a clause they like, +1 Trust and 0.35 goodwill.
- *Drop part of a clause*: an amendment the working group must still accept; if it does, +1 Trust and 0.5 goodwill.
- *A favour*: 2 Influence for a favour at the summit.

**Rival personalities** (`content.RIVAL_STYLES`, `rivals_move`). Each rival has a different one:
- *The Hawk*: the old behaviour; blocks first.
- *The Broker*: holds side events for the regional group it's closest to winning.
- *The Spoiler*: goes after the clauses you wrote, and saves one extra floor amendment for the summit.

Every turn an intelligence report on one rival reaches the Record (every rival with Rival intelligence).

**The Chief of Protocol** (`session-guide.js`). An original character: silver hair, half-moon glasses, a navy suit and a brass pin, in an oval brass frame. He has six expressions (calm, wry, eyebrow, concerned, delighted, thinking), blinks, breathes and reacts when his expression changes.
- *The walkthrough:* 18 steps, written for the player's own session: their country, instructions, rivals and crisis. It covers how the hour runs (a timeline), the four paths, the meters, the room, working a delegation, regional groups, the draft, the drafting table, rivals, crises/news/offers, skills, ending a turn, a preview of the Summit and of the debrief, then "Begin the session". A spotlight and the guide glide between the parts of the table (four dimming panels, because a huge box-shadow isn't painted inside the scaled stage). Back, Next, Skip, the arrow keys and Escape all work, and focus stays inside the guide. "Don't show the tutorial again" turns the guide off (Settings → The guide turns it back on), and Menu → Replay the walkthrough replays it.
- *Advice at key moments:* a new crisis, an offer at the door, a regional group won or lost, a rival striking your clause, intelligence, the chapter goal met or missed, the Summit opening, the first time your actions run out, and the first time you can afford a skill. One remark at a time, never over a card; it closes itself after 9 seconds.
- *The debrief* opens with his verdict.

The old one-off tips are gone.

## 21. Rivals that play to win, your staff, and the golden rule (phase 8, 5 Nov 2026)

Ben: "make sure there is counter play and that the cpu does try and win, while also remembering the golden rule of UN that the kids need to know - don't say no to something just because it is not exactly as you want it. accepting incremental change is better than no change at all." He kept rivals' plans hidden and added advisers: "advisors (with an icon) can be assigned to different countries to study, with better advisors giving more information, the ability to switch advisors, and advisors growing in strength when they successfully do something". His choices: junior advisers can be wrong and say how sure they are; advisers are hired with Influence, up to five, and bigger countries start with more but greener ones.

**Rivals plan** (`engine._rival_options`, `_rival_act`, `rivals_move`, `PLAN`).
- Each action, a rival lists every move it could make and values each by how much it serves its own instructions:
  - put its wish in the text (a clause card, 2.2);
  - take its red line out (an amendment 2.0, or a strike 1.8);
  - push the room on the battle clause, which is the clause carrying its red line, or else the whole text. A lobby is worth more the closer the delegation is to tipping.
- Other moves:
  - win back delegations you have turned (`poach`: your goodwill there falls by 0.15);
  - court a regional group;
  - sign up sponsors in the last chapter.
- Personalities weight the moves: the Hawk amends and strikes, the Broker holds side events and poaches, and the Spoiler strikes your clauses.
- By difficulty:
  - *Easy*: rivals pick a random move 40% of the time, and never poach.
  - *Standard*: rivals play the best move.
  - *Hard*: rivals also value going after the delegations you worked on last turn (+0.45).
- `projection()` is the vote on the whole draft without the hidden moods. The tug-of-war bar shows it.

**Your staff** (`start_staff`, `_staff_reports`, `_check_claims`, `a_hire`, `a_post`).
- **Starting staff:** a permanent member brings four junior advisers, a G20 member three (1, 1 and 2 stars) and any other delegation two seniors. That is four stars for everyone.
- **Kinds:**
  - *Analysts* read rivals.
  - *Regional experts* read their own group one star better.
  - *Legal advisers* say at the summit whether a floor amendment would pass.
  - The Intelligence network skill (formerly Rival intelligence) adds a star to every report.
- **Reports** start the turn after posting:
  - on a rival: a hunch about the region at one star, its next target at two, and the move plus its instructions at three;
  - on a delegation: its mood, then what it most wants, then who courts it and how it would vote.
- **Accuracy:** 70% at one star, 85% at two and 100% at three. Every report says how sure it is.
- **Experience** comes from a rival warning that proved right (the rival moved there, or your reserve stopped it), a warning you acted on, a delegation brought round, or a correct vote forecast. Promotions come at 3 and 7 experience.
- **Hiring:** 4 Influence for a junior, up to five advisers.

- *Right of reply* at the summit (Respect 5): answers a rival who speaks against your clause. Unanswered, the speech warms the room 0.12 towards that rival.

## 25. Talking to delegations, a quieter turn, and fewer numbers (phase 12, 5 Nov 2026)

Ben adopted everything from two rounds of lead-developer pushback and asked for it all in one phase, then a full test, then no more changes until his playtest.

**Pitches** (`talks.receptive`, `talks.a_lobby`).
- **The choice:** lobbying is a pitch of two arguments from five: economic interest, security and stability, shared values, law and precedent, and regional solidarity.
- **Who listens to what:** each delegation's strongest two land fully, the third half and the rest barely (1.0 / 0.5 / 0.15). The ranking is read from its World Bank dossier and situation, with a seeded shuffle.
  - Aid recipients and low-income countries respond to economic interest.
  - Permanent members and big economies respond to security.
  - Rich countries respond to values and precedent.
  - Developing countries respond to regional solidarity.
- **Effect:** a pitch moves the delegation 0.55 × (0.3 + 0.7 × its average match), replacing the flat Lobby step and the old bonus for having read its file.
- **Finding out what lands:** the file now shows "Pitch it: it listens to …", an adviser at two stars reports it, and every answer says which arguments landed.

**Haggling** (`talks.a_deal`: stages `open`, `offer`, `walk`).
- **Opening (2 actions):** you choose what you ask for. They name a price: what your asks cost them, less trust, plus seeded greed of 0.8–1.8, plus 0.6 if a rival is courting them. They also say which of your items they want.
- **Rounds (free):** offer less and they come down halfway, but never below their walk-away point. An offer below the walk-away point is an insult (−2 patience).
- **Patience:** 2 plus trust. Each round there's a chance they walk: 12% a round, plus 20% if a rival is courting them, less 5% per trust. A rival may also outbid you (+0.5).
- **Advisers:** one posted to the delegation reveals the walk-away point, exactly at three stars and as a range at two.
- **The rules carried over from phase 9:** the capital's objections, the budget, and deal votes holding.

**Compromise with rivals** (`talks.a_compromise`).
- **The same haggling,** offering "drop the commitment it can't accept" (worth 2.2) and/or "let its wish into the text" (1.6).
- **Prices:** a Broker asks about 1.6, a Spoiler 2.0 and a Hawk 2.8, so a Hawk needs both. The price is 0.6 less if the rival is losing the vote.
- **Peace:** it stops moving and amending, and its stance on the merged text is +1. Half its goodwill passes to you, and it co-sponsors.
- **Your capital** objects to dropping your own aims (−2 for the main aim, −1 for the second) and to letting your red line in (−3).

**Unlocks by stage** (`talks.UNLOCK`):
- **Bloc meetings:** posting and hiring advisers, the deal table, compromise.

- **Quick sessions:** everything at once.

**The turn-start reel** (`session-reel.js`). At the start of each turn the news comes one card at a time, with Next or "Skip the rest":

- every perk taxes balance;
- shops in a school game look like microtransactions.

Ben chose these three instead.

- **Your ambassador:** an avatar from the same respectful options as delegates. It is shown face to face in conversations and on your record. It is kept in the browser, because saving it to a student's account would be new student data and needs the school's sign-off.
- **Insignia:** cosmetic and earned, never bought (`content.INSIGNIA`):
  - First resolution;
  - Gold standard;
  - Hard-won;
  - The Council;
  - Word of honour;
  - Half a loaf;
  - Four paths;
  - Globetrotter.
- **Your approach:** chosen before each game. Each is a trade-off (`content.APPROACHES`):

  | Approach | Trade-off | Unlocked by |
  |---|---|---|
  | Career diplomat | none | always open |
  | Linguist | talks cost 1 less, opening a deal costs 1 more | silver on Standard or Hard |
  | Veteran | a senior adviser from the start, one action fewer in each of the first three turns | gold on Standard or Hard |
  | Envoy | up to three visitors and more offers; rivals gain 1 Influence more a turn | gold in three scenarios on Standard or Hard |

  The balance harness cycles the approaches and reports `approach_silver_plus`.

## 32. Deals that matter, more conversations, and the Summit as the UN runs it (phase 19, 8 Nov 2026)

From Ben's playtest. A Standard session took about 20 minutes, no stage lasted more than two turns, and the Summit "needs to feel like you're sitting in the UN chambers, and proper procedure followed".

**Pacing and staff.**
- **A stage lasts at least three turns** (`MIN_STAGE_TURNS`), however early the breakthrough.
- **Promotions are faster** (`PROMOTE = {2: 2, 3: 5}`), and an adviser posted to a delegation also earns experience when a talk with it goes well.

**Deals and betrayal.**
- **Betrayal** (`_betray`) is cutting what you promised (a clause, a pact or a peace), whether by striking, amending or voting for an amendment. It:
  - turns the delegation hostile;
  - cools its regional group;
  - costs 2 Standing;
  - voids its deal;
  - makes a rival furious;
  - adds to `rep["broken"]`, which makes every later deal dearer (+0.5 greed each).

  A rival's change to a clause you promised voids the promise; it no longer counts as your broken word (this was a bug).
- **Talk fades, deals bind** (`fade_talk`, `R["talk_fade"]` = 0.93 a turn). Goodwill from talking fades, except where a deal, a promise or a signed commitment holds the delegation.
- **The deal table also trades Influence** (you pay 3) **and Standing** (your public support costs 1; their praise gives 1).
- **Every deal is on the Agreements list in full** (`deal_log`).
- **Delegations bring trades that suit them** (`_trade_offer`): what they most want from you, for their vote if it's in play, otherwise for what they can give.

**More conversations.** Two to four visitors a turn, with new kinds (`visits._more`):
- an ultimatum (refuse it and the rival tables its amendment at the Summit: `g["threats"]`);
- a bloc bargain (`g["bargains"]`, kept or broken by the next stage's end);
- mediation;
- a leak in confidence (expose it and the source turns on you);
- old sins (the press asks about a broken word);
- an olive branch;
- your own staff asking to be posted.

**The Summit** (`campaign/summit.py`, `session-summit.js`) follows Ben's order of business:
1. **Position statements:** rivals and the strongest voices for and against, then yours (firm, open or bridge).
2. **The draft read in full.**
3. **A caucus:** moderated (you choose the topic and speak; rivals speak too) or unmoderated (a word with two delegations).
4. **Amendments, one at a time, as edits of the draft:** strike a clause, strike part of it, add a sub-clause, or soften or strengthen its verb on the ladder Invites / Encourages / Calls upon / Urges / Strongly urges.

   Each is debated, then voted. In the debate you can speak for or against, raise a point of order, claim a right of reply or accept the amendment as friendly. At the vote you can add a last offer or call in a favour.
5. **The final text,** with tracked changes.
6. **The final vote** on the text as a whole. There are no clause-by-clause votes.

**How amendments come forward.**
- **From real motives:**
  - a rival's red line or wish (a refused ultimatum heads the list);
  - a delegation's own red line in the text;
  - a supporter softening a clause that would fail, or strengthening one that is safe;
  - a champion adding a missing commitment.
- **Only contested ones** (yes within three of no). **One per delegation, five at most, plus up to two of your own.**
- **A settled matter is closed** (`closed`): nobody re-tables it.
- **A point of order succeeds only on a real ground:** an amendment that brings back what the working group struck.

The whip sheet's clause columns are scored against how each delegation would vote on each clause as finally amended.

## 33. Graded on all four, and the Architect path fixed (9 Nov 2026)

Ben: "all of them are being marked already, let's make it you can just go for all of them."

**No path to choose.**
- The setup's step 5 is now just your approach.
- Every session is graded on all four paths, and your result is your best (`result["chosen"]` is the best path; `grade` is its medal).
- The walkthrough has a step, "How each is judged", that gives the gold condition for each path (`content.PATHS[...]["gold"]`).
- Your secret instructions still decide Mandate.

**The Architect path, fixed.**
- **Why it failed:** gold needed 70% of the final text to be yours, but the zero draft's own clauses always make up much of the text. Even a strong drafting game ends near 50%.
- **The new rule:** gold now means the text bears your mark. You need at least three clauses of your own, at least half the text yours or amended by you (`architect_gold` = 0.5), and three in five voting yes.
- **Silver:** two clauses of your own and 40%.
- **Bronze:** 20%.
