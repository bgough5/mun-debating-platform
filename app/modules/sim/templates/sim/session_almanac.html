How a vote is decided

Every delegation has a position on each commitment a clause makes. In this scenario those positions are {{ "invented for practice: they are not any country's real position" if scn.fictional else "estimates from the sources in each country file below, not statements of fact" }}. The platform's vote model turns them into a delegation's likely position on a whole clause.

On top of that position, three things count:

  • Goodwill you and the rival delegations build by lobbying, promises and side events. It pulls a delegation towards whatever its friend supports.
  • A regional group's endorsement: when more than half of a group leans to one draft, the whole group leans a little further.
  • The mood on the day: a hidden draw for each delegation, fixed for the whole session, shared a little within blocs. It is the real uncertainty of any vote, and why the chance dial is a chance, not a promise.

A delegation votes yes when all of that adds up clearly for the text, no when clearly against, and abstains in between. {{ "In the Security Council, a no from any of the five permanent members is a veto." if scn_veto else "In the General Assembly, a simple majority of those voting passes a text; abstentions don't count either way." }}
